    LinkedIn Strategy

    Overview¶

    LinkedIn is the highest-reach, lowest-cost-per-post channel — but only if content is specific enough to be worth reading, per docs/departments/career-brand-coach.md's ≥2 posts/month KPI.

    Content Pillars¶

    Three recurring themes, matching the domains explicitly chosen for their QA-background advantage: 1. Fraud Detection — real project results, honest metrics, what the QA lens catches that a typical DS approach misses. 2. Anomaly Detection — same framing, tied to the flagship Data Quality Monitoring platform. 3. Data Quality Validation — the most direct QA-to-DS bridge; content here should feel like "obviously this person has 20 years of testing experience."

    Cadence¶

    Minimum 2 posts/month per the department KPI, ideally timed to real milestones (a project completion, a bootcamp phase finished, a genuine lesson learned) rather than posted on a schedule regardless of substance.

    Voice¶

    Specific over generic. A post that could have been written by anyone who read one article about the topic is a post not worth publishing. Every post should contain one concrete number, result, or example that couldn't be copy-pasted into someone else's post.

    What Not to Post¶

    • Unverified/unimplemented project claims (per projects/README.md's honest status tracking)
    • Generic "excited to announce" posts with no substance
    • Anything that reads as humble-bragging rather than genuinely useful

    Checklist¶

    • [ ] Post ties to one of the three content pillars
    • [ ] Contains at least one concrete, specific detail
    • [ ] Claims match actual project status honestly
    • [ ] Hook (first line) earns the scroll-stop

    Next Steps¶

    • Track which pillar gets the most engagement after a few months of posting; adjust emphasis accordingly.
