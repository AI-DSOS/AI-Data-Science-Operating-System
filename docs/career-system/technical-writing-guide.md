## Overview

Long-form writing is the deepest channel — it's where genuine technical depth gets demonstrated in a way a resume bullet or LinkedIn post can't. Quality over frequency matters more here than anywhere else in the career system.

## Article Selection Criteria

An article is worth writing when it has: a real project behind it, at least one honest result (including a negative/ceiling result, which often makes for a more interesting article than a clean win), and a lesson genuinely useful to someone else facing the same problem — not just a project writeup for its own sake.

## Structure Discipline

Follow `templates/technical-writing-guide-template.md`'s outline: hook, problem, approach (including what didn't work), honest results, lessons, call to action. The "what didn't work" section is often the most valuable and most commonly skipped — don't skip it.

## Publishing Cadence

≥1/quarter per `docs/departments/career-brand-coach.md`'s KPI — deliberately lower-frequency than LinkedIn, since article quality matters more than volume here.

## What Makes an Article Worth Reading

The car insurance project's honest ROC-AUC 0.65 result (against an 0.80 aspiration) is a better article topic than a project that hit its target cleanly — readers learn more from "here's what I tried and why it didn't fully work" than from a success story, and it demonstrates the honest-reporting discipline that's a genuine differentiator.

## Checklist

- [ ] Real project behind the article, not speculative
- [ ] Honest results included, even if imperfect
- [ ] "What didn't work" section present
- [ ] Structure follows the outline template
