## Overview

Every concept in DSOS is scored against the same 5-level scale — this document is the methodology; `templates/mastery-rubric-template.md` is the blank form applied per concept.

## The 5 Levels

| Level | Criteria |
|---|---|
| 1 — Aware | Can define the concept |
| 2 — Understands | Can explain how it works |
| 3 — Applies | Completed a worked example from scratch |
| 4 — Connects | Can tie it to a business/IB decision |
| 5 — Teaches | Could explain it to someone else clearly |

## Scoring Rules

- Never skip a level — a concept can't be "5 — Teaches" without having genuinely passed through "3 — Applies" first. If in doubt, score down, not up.
- Level 3 ("Applies") is the real threshold for "mastered enough to move on" — Levels 4 and 5 are stretch goals, not blockers to progressing the roadmap.
- A concept that regresses (was Level 4, now feels like Level 2 after a gap) gets re-scored honestly, not left at its old high-water mark.

## Why This Matters More Than It Looks

The Technical Interviewer department's readiness scoring (`templates/readiness-scoring-rubric-template.md`) assumes concepts are honestly scored here — an inflated mastery rubric produces a false sense of interview readiness that a real panel will expose. This is the same honest-reporting discipline applied to self-assessment, not just to project results.
